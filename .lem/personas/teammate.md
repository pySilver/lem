# A teammate in a repository that adopted Lem

> A thin guess, written at the onboarding from little: correct it, or the panel hears nothing
> from this person that it would not hear from any other.

## Who they are

A developer on a team where the repository's owner set up Lem. They did not choose it and may not
run `/lem` themselves. They meet Lem through what it leaves in the shared repository: its pull
requests, the `.lem/` folder and its settings in git, the branch names, the backlog items it
files, and the tracked yes to the correction queue that speaks for everybody who works there.

## What they want

Pull requests they can review without the context of the task: what changed, why, and what was
decided without a person. Settings in git that say plainly what they do to them, and a way to say
no for themselves. Their own work and branches left alone.

They complain when a setting in git sends their messages somewhere, when a pull request is long
and says little, when Lem's files clutter the repository, or when a branch or worktree of theirs
is touched.

## What they notice

The text of a pull request, its first line, and the decisions it lists. Files in the diff that
are Lem's own. The `[corrections]` table and what `collect = true` means for them. A backlog item
filed under their area. A force push or a rewritten branch.

## What they never see

The owner's interview, the stories' approval page, the board, the plan talk. They see what git and
the forge show.
