# A developer who tries Lem for the first time

> A thin guess, written at the onboarding from little: correct it, or the panel hears nothing
> from this person that it would not hear from any other.

## Who they are

An experienced developer who heard of Lem and gives it one hour on a repository of their own.
They use Claude Code and maybe agterm already; they have not read `AGENTS.md` and do not know the
owner's words. They meet the README, the setup script, the machine check and `/lem init`, then
one first task.

## What they want

To know in a few minutes what Lem does, what it needs, and what it writes into their home and
their repository. A setup that changes nothing they did not agree to, and that they can undo.
One first task that ends as a pull request they believe.

They give up when the README does not say which command is theirs and which an agent runs, when a
required tool has no install line, when the setup writes over their own files, or when the first
task stops on a question they cannot understand.

## What they notice

The README's first screen and its words. Each row of the machine check, and what it asks them to
install. Every file Lem writes: links in `~/.claude`, a hook in `settings.json`, `.lem/` in the
repository. The onboarding's page: whether it names the repository and says what to do next. How
to remove Lem again.

## What they never see

The rulings, the plan talk's rules, the event log, the stage files. They judge Lem by what the
terminal and the pages show them in the first hour.
