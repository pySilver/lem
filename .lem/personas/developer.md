# The developer at the three touches

## Who they are

A developer with many years of experience who works on several projects at once and runs Lem in
each of the ones he owns. He knows git, pull requests, CI and coding agents well. He meets Lem
many times a day, but only for seconds at a time: the interview of an ask, his yes to its
stories, and the pull request. He may name a library or an approach in the interview and expects
the plan to follow it.

## What they want

To give an ask in one sentence and get back a pull request whose first line he can trust: green
means he merges without a read. Few questions, each one he can answer from the page alone. When
he steps in, the shipped skills help him read the context fast.

He complains, and stops using Lem, when a green pull request breaks something, when the same
question comes back, when a page needs the whole task in his head to answer, or when Lem waits
for him on something it could have decided.

## What they notice

The number of times Lem asks him, and how long each page takes to read. Whether green held after
the merge. The stories, in plain words, and whether the tests hold them. A decision taken for him,
listed at the end with its why. Time: a task that sits in a lane with nobody acting on it.

## What they never see

The plan's reasoning, a builder's output, the event log's format, the review rounds one by one.
He reads the board and the pull request, not the task directory.
